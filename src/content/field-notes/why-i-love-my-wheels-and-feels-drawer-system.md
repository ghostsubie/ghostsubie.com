Before the drawers, packing the Ghost for a weekend looked like a yard sale in the trunk. Cooler wedged against the recovery bag, stove buried under the camp chairs, Jonah asking where the snacks went for the third time before we'd left the driveway.

The Wheels & Feels drawer system changed all of that in a single afternoon install. Two full-width drawers that turned the cargo area into a filing cabinet for camp life.

## What lives where

**Drawer one is the camp kitchen.** Stove, fuel, coffee kit, dry goods, utensils. Everything for a meal is in one pull. When we roll into camp at golden hour, dinner starts in minutes instead of after twenty minutes of trunk archaeology.

**Drawer two is recovery and tools.** Straps, shackles, tire repair, compressor, the stuff you hope you don't need but absolutely want organized when you do. There's a special kind of calm in knowing exactly where your recovery gear is.

**The top deck is Jonah's territory.** His bag, his fishing rod, the snacks he can reach himself. Giving a kid his own zone in the rig is the difference between "are we there yet" and a co-pilot.

## Why it works for a daily driver

Here's the part nobody talks about: Monday through Friday, the Ghost is a school-run car. The drawers sit flush, the trunk looks stock, and nothing rattles. Then Friday afternoon, we throw the camp bins on the top deck and we're gone. No repacking. No tetris. The system respects both lives the rig lives.

## The real reason I love it

It's not about the drawers. It's about what they buy: ten extra minutes at the campfire instead of digging through bags. A kid who can find his own headlamp. A calm setup when the weather's turning and you need camp up *now*.

Organization isn't the opposite of adventure. It's what makes room for it.
